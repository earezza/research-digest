How articles are ranked. Papers in leading journals rank highest, then specialist journals, then preprints. For today and this week, newer papers rank higher. For this month and longer, papers cited more often per month since publication rank higher, so a new paper is not judged against one that has had years to be cited. Each longer range includes everything the shorter ranges found. Papers whose abstract reports a concrete finding, such as a randomized trial, a first demonstration or a new atlas, get a small boost. Corrections, editorials and duplicates are removed.

The summary card beside each article is read from the paper's own abstract: its headline figure (two results compared, a percentage, a fold change, a hazard ratio or the study size), how the study was done, what was studied and how many. When the abstract gives no number, it shows the paper's main finding in the authors' words. Numbers are quoted as written; check the paper for context. All papers / Peer reviewed / Preprints filters every tab, field, search and category.

Searching. Keywords are looked for in titles and abstracts; put a phrase in quotes. A name on its own (Jennifer Doudna, Doudna JA) is searched both as an author and as keywords, with that author's papers first. author:, au: or by: searches authors only, for example author:"Feng Zhang", and combines with keywords: CRISPR author:Doudna. Author matches are checked against each paper's author list. Searches can be saved as categories.

Sources: 37 journals through Europe PMC (which indexes PubMed); preprints from bioRxiv, medRxiv and arXiv, read from their own services for today and this week and through Europe PMC for longer ranges. Open Sources to see the full list or limit what is read. No keys or accounts. Nothing is stored; each open reads the sources fresh. Preprints have not completed peer review. The grey line under each title is a sentence from the paper's own abstract.
